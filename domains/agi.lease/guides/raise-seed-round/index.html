How to Raise Your First Seed Round as an AI Founder

What actually moves a seed investor in the current AI market.

Share:X / TwitterLinkedIn

The seed market for AI is bifurcated: exceptional teams with a wedge close fast, everyone else grinds. This guide is about getting on the right side of that line.

Decide what you're actually raising on

There are three fundable stories at seed: a team investors already trust, a genuinely novel technical wedge, or early traction that's growing fast. You need at least one, told clearly. 'We're using AI to do X' is not a story — every deck says that.

Pick your strongest of the three and build the whole narrative around it. Don't dilute a strong wedge story with vanity metrics.

Build the list before you pitch

Warm intros still convert 5-10x cold outreach. Map second-degree connections to funds that have written AI seed checks in the last 12 months. Target 30-40 investors, not 300.

Run the raise as a process: soft-circle a lead first, then use momentum to fill the round in two to three weeks. A round that stays open for months signals a problem.

The deck that gets the second meeting

Ten to twelve slides: problem, why now, product, wedge/moat, traction, market, team, ask. Lead with the sharpest thing you have. If you have a working product with users, show the product early — don't bury it on slide nine.

Terms without getting fleeced

Most AI seed rounds are SAFEs at a cap. Know your comparable caps before you name a number. Prioritize the lead and clean terms over squeezing the last few points of valuation — a great investor at a fair price beats a mediocre one at a high one.

Key takeaways

  • Pick one of team / wedge / traction and build the whole story on it.
  • Warm intros to 30-40 active AI seed funds beat spraying 300 cold emails.
  • Soft-circle a lead, then close the round in weeks, not months.
